Starter guide: strength and protein during your GLP-1 treatment

General content to get started today, while we build the full programme.

Why muscle matters

Some of the weight lost during GLP-1 treatment may come from lean mass, not just fat. Preserving muscle during weight loss helps protect strength, mobility and long-term metabolism.

Strength training: how to start

No gym or prior experience needed. Consistency matters more than intensity.

1. Squat Hold a chair or wall for support if needed. 2-3 sets of 8-12 reps.
2. Press or push-up Standing against a wall, on the floor, or with dumbbells. 2-3 sets of 8-12 reps.
3. Row With a resistance band or dumbbells, pulling toward your body. 2-3 sets of 8-12 reps.
4. Plank or glute bridge Hold for 15-30 seconds, or 2-3 sets of 10-12 reps.
Start with 2 sessions a week. Increase frequency or load only once the previous level feels comfortable. On days with more nausea or fatigue, a short, gentle session beats none at all.

Protein: the basics

  • Spread protein across 3-4 meals a day instead of concentrating it in one.
  • Common sources: eggs, Greek yoghurt or skyr, chicken, fish, legumes, tofu, protein powder.
  • If your appetite is low, prioritise protein first on your plate and try liquid options (shakes, drinkable yoghurts), which are often easier to get down.

When to talk to your doctor or registered dietitian

  • If you're losing weight very quickly and consistently.
  • If you notice a noticeable loss of strength.
  • If nausea or low appetite are intense or persistent.
  • Before starting to train, if you have any relevant medical condition.
This guide offers general, non-personalised information. It does not replace advice from your doctor, endocrinologist or registered dietitian, and it is not medical or dosing guidance. Do not change your medication or dose based on this content.
